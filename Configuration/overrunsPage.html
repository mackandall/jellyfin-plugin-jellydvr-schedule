When a live game runs late, the shows after it on that channel start late too. The score is followed on ESPN's scoreboard, and shortly before each of those recordings starts its end is moved later. Jellyfin fixes a recording's length when it starts, so that is the last moment it can change. A recording of the game itself gets extra time for overtime, and a follow-up recording if it's still going.

In the guides (web and JDVR)

  1. A recording is watched when a game (two teams in the guide title, such as “49ers at Cowboys”) is on its channel within the look-back time before it, or when the recording is a game.
  2. The game is matched on ESPN's public scoreboard by the two teams and its start time. NFL, college football, NBA, WNBA, college basketball, NHL, MLB and the main soccer leagues are covered.
  3. Three minutes before the recording starts, its end moves later if the channel won't be back on schedule in time: the time the game still needs, plus the network's coverage after the final (section 3), plus the margin. Postgame shows listed in the guide count towards that coverage.
  4. No match on the scoreboard: the sport's fixed extra time is used instead.
